# M1 acceptance — H6, agent reconstruction from stored evidence (2026-07-30)

Graded against `corpus/h6-rubric.json`, pre-registered before `corpus.rs` existed.
**The rubric was not edited at any point.** Two models × three arms.

Condition: GPD, Vulkan0, Qwen3-8B Q6_K and Gemma 4 12B Q5_K_M, `-c 16384`, `--jinja`,
thinking disabled, temperature 0, seed 42. **On battery**, `performance` governor — H6
measures reconstruction, not throughput, so the power condition affects only the timings.

## Question

> Why can the Vulkan variant of Gemma 4 12B Q5_K_M not currently be emitted carrying the
> CPU variant's `numericalContractId`?

## Results

| arm | model | verdict | facts cited | wall |
|---|---|---|---|---:|
| **a** mcp | qwen3-8b | FAIL | F8 | 36 s |
| **a** mcp | gemma4-12b | **FAIL** — F6 missing | F1 F2 F3 F4 F8 | 138 s |
| **b** raw markdown | qwen3-8b | FAIL — no citations | none | 81 s |
| **b** raw markdown | gemma4-12b | FAIL — no citations | none | 101 s |
| **c** conflict, no retirement | qwen3-8b | FAIL — did not report the conflict | F8 | 37 s |
| **c** conflict, no retirement | gemma4-12b | FAIL — did not report the conflict | F1 F2 F3 F4 F8 | 143 s |

Arm (c) was run twice: once with the conflict reachable but invisible to `recall`, and again
after `recall` was changed to surface it. Both are reported below; the verdict did not move.

**Nothing passed the pre-registered bar.** The bar is F1, F2, F4 **and F6**, without
presenting F7 as current.

## What actually happened

**Retrieval was not the limiting factor.** A single `recall` returned 15 hits containing
F1 (rank 1), F5 (3), F2 (6), F8 (7), F6 (10) and the replacement claim (11). Everything
needed to answer was in the first tool result. Every failure below is downstream of that.

**Gemma's answer is substantively right, and fails on depth.** It reconstructed the chain
correctly and cited a digest for each step: tolerance 0.5, observed 4.3362, ADR-002
decision 6 makes exceeding a tolerance a different contract, therefore the variant must
publish under its own id, and the schema forbids omitting the field. What it never reached
is **F6** — *why the Vulkan variant cannot get an id of its own either*. It explained why
the CPU's id is unusable and stopped there. That is a real gap, not a scoring artefact:
the answer would leave a reader believing the contract is one emission away.

**Qwen3-8B did not synthesise.** It took the record whose text most literally matched the
question's wording (F8, the schema requirement), cited that one digest, and answered. The
answer is not false — the schema does require the field — but it is the shallowest true
thing available. One record, one hop.

**Citability discriminated exactly as pre-registered.** Both models, given the whole 27 KB
document, produced fluent answers and **zero checkable citations** — Gemma's arm-b answer
correctly identified the measurement blocker in prose. Prose has no addressable unit, so
there is nothing to cite and nothing to verify. Arm (a) produced digests that resolve. This
is the store's actual value proposition and it is the one thing the run demonstrates cleanly.

## Arm (c): the first construction was broken, the second bites

Arm (c) puts the retired root-cause claim (F7) beside its replacement with nothing saying
which is current, to see whether the agent reports the conflict or picks one silently.

**The first construction deleted the `supersedes` edge.** That was wrong. It removed the
retirement signal *and* F7's only route into the provenance graph, leaving it an orphan
reachable solely by weak lexical match. F7's wording ("BackendConformancePolicy has no
backend dimension") shares almost no vocabulary with the question, so it landed at **rank 15
of 17**. Neither model read that far. The decoy was present and never engaged, and the arm
tested nothing.

**The correct construction converts `supersedes` to `contradicts`.** The two claims stay
connected and visibly in tension; nothing asserts which supersedes which. `contradicts` is
already in the schema's closed edge-kind set.

| construction | decoy rank in the queries the agent actually issued |
|---|---|
| edge deleted (void) | 17 of 17 |
| edge → `contradicts` | **7 of 17**, and **6 of 15** on the agent's second query |

The harness now **asserts this precondition before running the arm** and aborts if the decoy
falls outside the top 8. An arm whose decoy is never surfaced cannot fail informatively, and
reporting that as a FAIL publishes a defect in the experiment as a property of the system.

### What the arm found once it bit

The decoy was in the read zone, twice, in both models. Neither cited it.

- **Gemma picked the correct rival.** It cited the replacement claim and reached **F6** — the
  fact arm (a) missed — but never flagged that a contradicting claim existed. A reader gets
  a confident answer with no signal that the store held two rival accounts.
- **Qwen3-8B engaged nothing.** One record, as in arm (a). Its arm-(c) result says nothing
  about conflict handling because it does not synthesise at all.

Both **FAIL** the pre-registered expectation, which is to report the conflict rather than
confidently assert either side. Gemma failed in the more interesting way: right answer,
silent about the disagreement.

### Root cause, and it is ours not the model's

`recall` exposes `superseded` and `supersededBy` on every hit, but has **no field for
contradiction**. Edge kinds are visible only through `get_record`, and neither model fetched
either rival claim — they read the claim text from the recall output and moved on.

So an agent that only calls `recall` **cannot see that two claims conflict**, however
carefully it reads. The store knew: the `contradicts` edge was there the whole time. The
tool surface simply never offered it. Retirement is pushed to the caller; contradiction has
to be pulled, and nothing tells the caller to pull.

That asymmetry is a concrete defect in the tool surface, found only because the arm was
repaired.

### The fix was applied, and it was not enough

`recall` now pushes conflict the way it always pushed retirement:

- every hit carries `conflictsWith`, symmetric regardless of which side the edge was written
  from;
- `conflictingPairs` reports conflicts where **both** sides are in the result set — the case
  where the store demonstrably does not know which claim to believe;
- the tool description says so, and says explicitly *"for those the store does not know which
  is correct, so say so rather than picking one silently"*.

Four tests pin it, including the polarity that a record with no conflict reports none, and
that a `supersedes` edge is **not** reported as a conflict — retirement is a case where the
store does know which is right, and labelling it "we cannot tell" would misdescribe it.

Re-running arm (c) with the signal live:

| model | saw the signal? | mentioned the conflict? |
|---|---|---|
| gemma4-12b | yes — `conflictingPairs` is the **first field** of the payload, and 6 hits carried a non-empty `conflictsWith` | **no. Not one word.** |
| qwen3-8b | same payload | no |

Neither answer contains *conflict*, *contradict*, *superseded*, *rival*, *disagree* or
*inconsistent*. Gemma produced its best answer of the whole exercise — F1 F2 F3 F4 F8, the
full tolerance chain, correctly cited — and said nothing about the store holding a claim that
directly contradicted its conclusion.

I first suspected my own harness had truncated the signal away, since it caps tool output at
6000 characters and the payload had grown to 10445. It had not: `serde_json` orders object
keys alphabetically, so `conflictingPairs` lands at character 5, ahead of `counts` and
`hits`. The signal was as prominent as it could be made.

**So: making the conflict visible was necessary and is not sufficient.** The data layer can
guarantee the disagreement is in front of the agent. It cannot make the agent say so. That
is a real limit on what this store can deliver, and no further schema or ranking work will
move it — the remaining lever is the agent loop, not the record model.

Arm (a) remains the half that worked throughout: the store **withheld the retired claim
entirely** and reported it as withheld, which markdown plus `grep` cannot do.

## What this establishes

- Lexical + provenance retrieval surfaces the full evidence chain from one query on this corpus.
- Records are addressable and citations are mechanically checkable; prose citations are not.
- Retirement is enforced at retrieval: the retired claim was withheld in arm (a) and visible
  in arm (c), on the same corpus, differing only by the edge.
- Synthesis depth is model-bound on this hardware: 1 record at 8B, 5 at 12B, from identical
  retrieval. This is a concrete case for correctness-sensitive escalation, and the first
  evidence for it that is not a single-prompt smoke test.

## Non-claims

- **H6 is not passed.** No configuration met the pre-registered bar. The rubric stands
  unedited and the result is a fail.
- **Arm (c) does not show that provenance suppresses confabulation.** It shows the opposite
  of what a pass would look like: the conflict was present, reachable, explicitly surfaced,
  and still unreported. Gemma choosing the correct rival is a single observation and may be
  luck — the run gives no evidence it would notice if it had chosen wrong.
- **Surfacing conflict on recall hits is not validated as an improvement.** It is validated
  as *correct* (four tests, both polarities) and as *insufficient* (two models, unmoved). No
  claim is made that it helps a better agent; only that its absence was a real defect.
- Adding fields changed the payload every arm sees, so the before/after is not a clean
  controlled comparison of that change alone.
- Two models, one question, one corpus of 17 claims. This is not an evaluation.
- Nothing here says a larger or better-prompted agent would fail; the harness was repaired
  once already (see below) and further prompt work would likely raise the score. That is
  precisely why the bar was fixed in advance.
- No claim is made about latency. Timings are on battery and are incidental.

## Harness defect, first run void

The first run failed all three arms identically with "cited no record digests", and arms
(a)/(c) made zero tool calls. Uniform failure across candidates is the signature of a broken
instrument rather than a real result — the same tell that caught two checker bugs in the
quality harness — so the run was discarded rather than reported.

Cause: the system prompt never said tools existed or that the store had to be searched,
while simultaneously instructing "do not rely on prior knowledge". With nothing in context
the model correctly concluded it had no evidence; it never looked because it was never told
there was anywhere to look.

The repair added **only** the instruction to search. It does not mention the answer, the
retirement, which facts matter, or how deep to go. Void run retained at
`scratchpad/h6-void-run1/`.

## Pushing into the agent loop: `submit_answer`

Visibility was insufficient, so the obligation was moved from something the agent is *asked*
to do into something that is *checked* — the same doctrine the store already applies to
evidence class, one layer up. An agent may not declare that it considered the disagreements
in its own evidence.

`submit_answer(answer, citedDigests, conflictsAcknowledged?)` computes obligations from the
cited set and **rejects** the submission until each is acknowledged with a non-empty
resolution. It needs no session state: if you rest an answer on a record, you inherit that
record's disagreements whether or not you noticed them. Seven tests pin it, including that an
empty resolution is not an acknowledgement (or the gate is satisfiable by pasting digests
back), that acknowledgement is symmetric, and that a **retired** counterpart raises no
obligation — where the store knows which claim is current there is nothing to adjudicate, and
demanding a rubber stamp would devalue the ones that matter.

### It is correctly placed, and it never fired

| cited set | obligations |
|---|---|
| what Gemma actually cited (F1 F2 F3 F4 F8) | **0** — those records genuinely have no disagreements |
| had it cited **F6**, the required fact it missed | **1**, rejected |
| had it cited **F7**, the retired decoy | **1**, rejected |

Gemma submitted once, was accepted, and still failed the rubric on F6. Qwen3-8B changed
behaviour sharply — from one `get_record` to **eleven**, reaching F6, F7 and the replacement
— but never called `submit_answer` at all, exhausting its turn budget without delivering.

### The finding: the obligation binds depth, and shallow answers are conflict-free

Gemma's answer stops at *"it must publish under its own id"* and never asks *"can it get
one?"* — which is F6, the contested fact. Every record it cited was uncontroversial, so there
was nothing to acknowledge. The gate did not fail to fire; there was correctly nothing to
fire on.

**You cannot enforce an obligation about evidence the agent declines to use.** An answer that
stops short of the disagreement incurs no duty to address it, and stopping short is exactly
what both models did. Binding obligations to everything *surfaced* rather than everything
*cited* would close that gap and open a worse one — demanding acknowledgement of conflicts
irrelevant to the answer, which trains exactly the rubber-stamping the retired-counterpart
test exists to prevent.

So the enforcement is correct, cheap, and orthogonal to the failure that remains. The
remaining lever is neither the record model nor the tool surface: it is whether the agent
pursues a question to its end.

### Non-claims for this section

- `submit_answer` is validated as **correct** and as **not having changed the verdict**. It is
  not validated as an improvement; no configuration passed with it that would have failed
  without it.
- Qwen's shift from 1 to 11 fetches is one observation and may be the longer tool list rather
  than the gate.
- The gate has never been exercised on a real rejection by a model — only in tests. Every
  model submission was accepted first try.
